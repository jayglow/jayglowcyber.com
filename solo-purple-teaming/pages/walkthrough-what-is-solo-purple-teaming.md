<h1 id="walkthrough-what-is-solo-purple-teaming">Walkthrough: What is

<p>As I advanced in my career as a defensive cybersecurity professional,
I quickly realized that building effective detections, hunting
sophisticated threats, and responding to incidents required more than
just a defensive mindset—it demanded a deep understanding of offensive
tactics. To close that gap, I trained myself in offensive security,
learning to think and operate like an attacker.</p>
<p>Along the way, I developed a structured process that led to real
defensive wins. This course is the result of that experience—a method I
call <strong>solo purple teaming</strong>.</p>

<h2 id="layout"><strong>Layout</strong></h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Define Scope</strong></li>
<li><strong>Identify Tactics, Techniques, and Procedures (TTPs) to
research</strong></li>
<li><strong>Use real-world experiences or research to guide your
starting point</strong></li>
<li><strong>Focus on a few TTPs at a time for deeper
analysis</strong></li>
<li><strong>Build Attack Scenario</strong></li>
<li><strong>Create a realistic scenario to test in your
lab</strong></li>
<li><strong>Develop an attack path based on selected TTPs</strong></li>
<li><strong>Build and configure the lab environment
accordingly</strong></li>
<li><strong>Map out the complete attack path</strong></li>
<li><strong>Plan RED Team Actions</strong></li>
<li><strong>Consider each offensive move carefully</strong></li>
<li><strong>Evaluate existing defenses and explore creative
bypasses</strong></li>
</ul>
<h2 id="execute">Execute</h2>
<ul>
<li>Launch your attack scenario in the lab</li>
<li>Repeat until the attack is both successful and undetected</li>
<li>Take notes on detection triggers, failures, and bypasses</li>
<li>Adjust tooling, techniques, or timing as needed</li>
<li>This is an iterative, hands-on learning process Failures are
expected and valuable for refining both attack and defense</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="explore">Explore</h2>
<ul>
<li>Identify Telemetry Gaps</li>
<li>Identify Artifacts of the attack</li>
<li>Identify Indicators of Compromise</li>
<li>Start identifying detection opportunities</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="trace">Trace</h2>
<ul>
<li>Reverse engineering payloads</li>
<li>Static and Dynamic Malware Analysis</li>
<li>Enable advanced auditing where possible</li>
</ul>
<h1 id="solo-purple-teaming-is-iterative">Solo Purple Teaming is
Iterative</h1>
<p>Once you've completed your initial run-through—executed the attack,
explored the telemetry, and built some detections—it’s time
to <strong>start the process over.</strong></p>
<p>This next phase is about <strong>iteration and growth</strong>. Now
that you've established a foundation, start layering on <strong>new
TTPs</strong>—add lateral movement, privilege escalation, persistence
mechanisms, or exfiltration. The idea is to simulate a
more <strong>realistic and complete attack chain</strong> over time.</p>
<p>As you evolve the scenario, your goal is also to <strong>test and
challenge your own defenses</strong>. Can you bypass the detections you
just implemented? What if you slightly modify your technique or use a
different tool? This is where the real value of solo purple teaming
starts to emerge—you begin to think both like an attacker and a
defender, adapting and learning on both sides of the engagement.</p>
<p>Don't worry if this feels like a lot right now—<strong>this will all
make sense as we walk through it step-by-step</strong>. The beauty of
this methodology is that it’s not about getting it perfect on the first
try. It’s about continuous improvement, creative problem-solving, and
ultimately developing a more resilient security posture.</p>
<p>So as we move forward, think of this not as a linear process, but
a <strong>feedback loop</strong>—one that strengthens both your
offensive intuition and your defensive strategy every time you run
it.</p>
